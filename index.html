Research user study

Comparing two video-editing methods

You'll watch a short input video together with a one-sentence edit request, then compare two edited versions and tell us which one follows the request best.

How it works

  1. 1 Read the edit request and watch the original input video.
  2. 2 Watch Option A and Option B — two edited versions, shown in random order.
  3. 3 For each option, tick the box if it actually performs the requested edit.
  4. 4
    Pick the option you think is better overall, judged on:
    • ✓ Did it happen? The requested motion actually occurs.
    • ✨ How natural? The more natural and visually appealing the motion looks, the better.
    • ⚡ How strong? A strong, obvious motion beats a slight one you can barely notice.

    Please avoid "About the same" unless the two videos are truly indistinguishable on every point above — use it sparingly.

About the audio: this study is only about motion editing. Sound is included purely for reference/context — please don't judge or score a video based on its audio or sound quality.

There are 17 scenarios. It takes about 8–12 minutes.

Your privacy

Your responses are completely anonymous — we do not collect your name, email, or any other personal information. We only record your choices on the video samples shown, which will be combined with everyone else's responses into aggregate statistics.
